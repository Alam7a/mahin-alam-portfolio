        How it works.

        How to use

        • Countries: pick a country. Compare at the bottom.
        • Scenario designer: add up to 6 road sections. For each, pick geometry, weather and skill. Read the idea, numbers and calculations. Download the run sheet.
        • Calculators: convert gaps or check lane lengths.

        Example

        Open the designer and press "Load Sc1". Tap Section 1. At 110 km/h the T2 lead gap of 3.37 s is 103.0 m. Try an invalid value: type "abc" as the lane length and read the message.

        Method

        • Geometries, weather levels, traffic types, skills and named scenarios come from the project's scenario workbook. Nothing is invented.
        • Closest named scenario: same geometry first, then most shared traffic, then matching weather.
        • Gap in metres = speed (km/h) ÷ 3.6 × time (s).
        • Average acceleration = (v₂² − v₁²) ÷ (2 × L), speeds in m/s.
        • Lateral acceleration = v² ÷ R. Stopping distance = v × t + v² ÷ (2a). Work-zone taper L = W × S ÷ 1.6.

        Assumptions

        • Constant speed in a gap conversion. Constant acceleration over the whole lane.
        • Reaction time, lane-start speed, ramp entry speed and sight range are your inputs. The sheet gives none of them.
        • Merge and lane-change gap means come from a PoliTO dissertation, not Ontario data.
        • The figure is a schematic and is not to scale.

        Limits

        • Not legal or safety advice, and not an official requirement of any authority.
        • Country data was compiled up to 9 November 2026. Some items rest on summaries or are unverified, and are labelled.
        • Pass or fail limits are not set.
        • Anything with no value in the sheet is listed as skipped, not guessed.
        • Three evidence rows in the sheet carry older geometry IDs. They are mapped to the current IDs (weaving to G7, work zone to G8, bridge to G10).
        • Not scientifically validated.
